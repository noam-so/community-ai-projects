## Principles fit

Publicly owned rather than community owned: Oak and the DfE hold the off switch, and teachers use and test it but hold no stake. The need is real and evidenced, though defined by Oak and the DfE rather than with teachers, pupils or parents. Openness is strong, with a public repository, a gov.uk transparency record and a published safety approach, while accountability is thinner, as contributions are closed and no safety figures are published. Free to every teacher, MIT code, Open Government Licence content, set against per-token spend leaving for US model providers. Nothing published on cost, energy or carbon.
